# Independent-truth distributed inverse validation

## The question

Do the distributed inverse results survive when synthetic truth is no longer
generated by the same spatial grid, time integrator, or three-knot property
representation used for inference? How much of the earlier PINN-versus-
conventional difference remains after both estimators receive the same explicit
coefficient-smoothness penalty?

This experiment is the first ThermoTwin distributed inverse study that removes
the exact numerical and constitutive-basis inverse crime. It remains synthetic
and still assumes the same continuum thermoelectric equations. It is not
hardware validation.

## Independent truth

The established inference model is cell-centred finite volume with five cells,
classical RK4, and a piecewise-linear resistivity curve at 285, 300, and 315 K.
The new truth generator instead uses:

- 25 node-centred temperatures, including both physical faces;
- independently assembled heat fluxes and voltage quadrature;
- transition-split third-order SSPRK integration with a 0.00025 s step; and
- a smooth cubic electrical-resistivity law.

With

```text
z = (T - 300 K) / 15 K,
```

the truth law is

```text
rho_truth(T) = 1e-5 * [
    1.07 - 0.0516 z - 0.0296 z^2 + 0.03 z (z^2 - 1)
] ohm m.
```

The cubic correction is zero at all three inference knots. Consequently, the
truth retains the earlier knot multipliers

```text
(1.04, 1.07, 1.03)
```

while having a between-knot shape that no three-knot piecewise-linear fit can
represent exactly. This separates knot agreement from recovery of the actual
continuous curve.

Both numerical models implement the same declared 1-D thermoelectric continuum
physics. Numerical independence is therefore a model-discrepancy test, not a
claim that either discretization is experimental truth.

## Data split

Each inverse fit sees noisy face temperatures and terminal voltage from three
constant-current truth experiments:

1. zero-current relaxation across a 20 K reservoir lift;
2. positive 0.8 A across a 10 K lift; and
3. negative 0.8 A across a 10 K lift.

The declared independent Gaussian noise is 0.01 K for each face temperature
and 10 microvolts for voltage. Samples are separated by 0.08 s.

After fitting, each resistivity curve is frozen and transferred through the
established finite-volume solver to three excluded experiments:

| Holdout | Purpose | Included in pass/fail? |
| --- | --- | :---: |
| positive 0.4 A, 20 K lift | in-support constant-current transfer | yes |
| negative 0.8 A pulse, 20 K lift | switched-current transfer | yes |
| positive 0.4 A, 40 K lift | outside the 285--315 K inference-knot interval | no; diagnostic only |

The pulse switches on at 0.08 s and off at 0.28 s. It can be used for frozen-
curve forward prediction even though the current distributed PINN cannot yet
train directly across a current discontinuity.

The 40 K case intentionally evaluates constant endpoint extrapolation outside
the 285--315 K inference basis. It is not treated as evidence about the material
law beyond that interval.

## Paired estimator comparison

Four estimators receive identical observations and starting multipliers in each
trial:

1. conventional, no explicit property penalty;
2. conventional, matched explicit penalty;
3. inverse PINN, no explicit property penalty; and
4. inverse PINN, matched explicit penalty.

The two PINN variants use the same neural initialization within a trial. The
shared explicit roughness is

```text
R(theta) = mean[(theta[i+2] - 2 theta[i+1] + theta[i])^2],
```

where `theta` is the vector of log multipliers. The matched weight is 25. The
same implementation computes this term for both estimator families. The
conventional Gauss--Newton step includes equivalent pseudo-residuals, so it is
optimizing the regularized objective rather than merely using the penalty in a
final line search.

This aligns the explicit coefficient penalty. It cannot align the PINN's
implicit neural bias, its representation of the hidden field, or its PDE loss.
The comparison is therefore fairer than the earlier unregularized baseline,
but it is not an equivalence theorem between estimator classes.

## Predeclared gate

An estimator passes a trial only if its in-support curve and both in-support
holdouts satisfy every limit:

Here, `in-support` means inside the declared 285--315 K inference-knot
interval. It does not imply that the noisy training trajectories sample every
temperature in that interval equally.

| Metric | Limit |
| --- | ---: |
| Maximum relative resistivity error over 285--315 K | 0.10 |
| Cold-face temperature RMSE | 0.03 K |
| Hot-face temperature RMSE | 0.03 K |
| Hidden internal-temperature RMSE | 0.03 K |
| Terminal-voltage RMSE | 50 microvolts |
| Maximum absolute temperature error | 0.08 K |
| Prediction solver energy residual | 1e-10 W |

The energy residual remains a finite-volume solver-integrity check. It is near
roundoff by construction and is not independent evidence of correct property
recovery.

### What matching the penalty changed

For the conventional estimator, the explicit curvature penalty reduced mean
in-support voltage error from 32.3 to 18.7 microvolts, but did not improve the
trial pass count or continuous property error. The reason is visible in the
coefficients: a second-difference penalty suppresses curvature but permits a
large nearly linear slope in log resistivity. Weak endpoint information can
therefore still produce the wrong curve.

For the PINN, adding the same penalty barely changed the estimates. Both PINN
variants already produced nearly log-linear curves. The earlier stability is
therefore not explained by the explicit second-difference term alone. It may
come from the hidden-field representation, PDE coupling, implicit neural bias,
or their interaction. This experiment does not separate those mechanisms.

The correct conclusion is limited:

> Across three paired synthetic model-mismatch trials, both implemented PINN
> variants recovered a stable effective resistivity curve and transferred
> through two excluded in-support regimes. Matching the explicit curvature
> penalty did not make the conventional estimator equally stable, and it also
> did not explain the PINN's stability.

It is not correct to conclude from three trials that PINNs generally outperform
regularized conventional inverse methods.

## Transfer metrics

Mean hidden-field errors remain small for every estimator, reinforcing that
temperature alone is weakly discriminating for resistivity in these regimes.
Voltage and continuous property error are the more informative comparisons.

| Estimator | 20 K constant internal RMSE | 20 K pulse internal RMSE | 40 K diagnostic voltage RMSE |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Conventional, unregularized | 0.000788 K | 0.002360 K | 69.596 microvolts |
| Conventional, matched | 0.000838 K | 0.002495 K | 64.828 microvolts |
| PINN, unregularized | 0.000678 K | 0.002189 K | 46.111 microvolts |
| PINN, matched | 0.000678 K | 0.002189 K | 46.519 microvolts |

The outside-support result is reported but not passed or failed. The inference
model uses constant endpoint extrapolation, while the cubic truth continues to
change. Good performance there would be convenient numerical transfer, not
evidence that the inferred material curve is correct outside its support.

## Reproduce

Install the optional PINN and report dependencies, then run:

```bash
thermotwin-distributed-independent \
  --trials 3 \
  --epochs 600 \
  --first-seed 47001 \
  --truth-nodes 25 \
  --truth-time-step 0.00025 \
  --smoothness-weight 25
```

The equivalent module command is:

```bash
python3 -m thermotwin.distributed_independent_validation
```

The generated figure is written to
`thermotwin/figures/DISTRIBUTED_INDEPENDENT_VALIDATION/distributed_independent_validation.png`;
the source data are saved beside it as JSON, and both are ignored by
Git.

## Remaining limits

- Three trials are too few for a failure-rate estimate or interval coverage.
- Both models still use the same continuum equations and exactly known boundary
  parameters.
- Only electrical resistivity is inferred; Seebeck coefficient and thermal
  conductivity are fixed correctly.
- Sensor noise is independent Gaussian noise without bias, lag, missingness, or
  correlated calibration errors.
- Only one explicit prior family and weight are compared.
- The PINN's implicit regularization remains unmatched.
- The truth law is synthetic rather than an independently measured material.
- The distributed PINN still cannot train directly on switched-current data.

Two follow-ons now use this independent truth. The
[observation-sufficiency study](DISTRIBUTED_OBSERVATION_IDENTIFIABILITY.md)
distinguishes exact zero-current structural blindness from practical
sensor-noise limits. The
[profile and coverage audit](DISTRIBUTED_PROFILE_COVERAGE.md) adds
shrinkage-plus-curvature, slope-aware conventional multistart fits,
representative fixed-coefficient nonlinear profiles, and 20 repeated local
interval trials. A larger campaign would still be needed for precise
failure-rate or coverage estimates.
