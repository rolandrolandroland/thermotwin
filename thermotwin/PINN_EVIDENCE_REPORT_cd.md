# Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Thermal State and Property Inference

**What the governing equations contribute, and what no loss curve can certify**

ThermoTwin technical report. All results are synthetic.

---

## Abstract

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) are often justified by the claim that
embedding governing equations makes a model learn physics rather than fit data.
We test that claim in two controlled synthetic studies on thermoelectric
systems, then test whether the resulting models can be trusted on their own
diagnostics. In the first study, two networks with identical architecture,
identical initial weights, and the same 56 noisy exchanger-temperature readings
reconstruct a transient in which both sensors are absent between 17 and 23 s.
Only one receives the four node energy balances. The physics-informed network
reduces error in the missing interval by 87.86%, error on the never-instrumented
module faces by 99.68%, and whole-system energy-rate imbalance by 99.19%,
winning every metric in 5 of 5 paired trials — while the data-only network fits
the retained noisy rows slightly *better* and trains 3.8x faster. In the second
study, an inverse PINN and a regularized conventional estimator infer a
temperature-dependent electrical resistivity curve inside a 1-D distributed leg
from terminal measurements, with synthetic truth generated by a different
discretization, integrator, and property basis. Across the same ten paired
trials, the PINN's mean curve error is 1.7537% versus 4.6639%, lower in 9 of 10
trials. That curve error is evaluated over 285–315 K, while fitted trajectories
span only about 294.83–305.21 K; roughly two-thirds of the evaluation interval
is therefore outside the training-temperature range. On a withheld operating
condition its mean voltage error is 8.6405 µV versus 11.0188 µV, but lower in
only 5 of 10 trials. Two reliability audits then show the limits: a falling
observation loss does not imply a converged physical residual, and a rejected,
non-identifiable observation set produced a *better-looking* fit than the
identifiable one. We conclude that physics constraints deliver large, repeatable
gains on unmeasured quantities, and that they must be paired with separate
pre-fit and post-fit gates because fit quality alone cannot detect either
failure mode.

## 1. Introduction

Two recurring problems in hardware characterization are that the quantity you
care about is not the quantity you can measure, and that the measurements you
can take are incomplete. Module face temperatures sit inside a stack;
temperature-dependent material properties are functions, not scalars; sensors
drop out exactly when the interesting transient happens.

A physics-informed neural network addresses both by representing the state as a
differentiable function of time (and space) and penalizing violation of the
governing equations in addition to mismatch with data. The appeal is that the
equations constrain the solution where data are absent. The risk is that the
resulting model produces a low loss, a smooth curve, and no indication of
whether either is meaningful.

This report separates those two questions:

1. **Does the physics measurably help?** Sections 3.1 and 4.1 compare matched
   networks that differ only in whether the equations enter the loss.
   Sections 3.2 and 4.2 compare an inverse PINN against a strengthened
   conventional estimator on a function-valued unknown.
2. **Can the result be trusted without knowing the answer?** Sections 3.3, 4.4
   and 4.5 audit training convergence and observation sufficiency, and find two
   distinct failure modes invisible to the loss.

## 2. Background

**Forward and inverse problems.** A forward problem maps known parameters to
predicted observables. An inverse problem infers parameters or fields from
observations, and is typically ill-posed: many parameter values fit the data
within noise.

**PINN construction.** A network predicts the state; automatic differentiation
supplies its time and space derivatives; a residual term penalizes the
difference between those derivatives and the ones the governing equations
require. Data terms are added where observations exist. For the lumped
four-node model the residual is the four ODE balances; for the distributed leg
it is the conservative PDE plus the two dynamic face-node balances.

**Inverse crime.** If synthetic truth is generated with the same discretization
and basis the estimator assumes, the comparison flatters the estimator. The
second study avoids this by generating truth on a 25-node spatial model with
SSPRK3 integration and a smooth cubic resistivity law, while inference retains
a finite-volume model with three property knots.

**Practical identifiability.** Even a well-posed model can be unidentifiable
from a given observation set: the sensitivity directions that distinguish
parameters may be smaller than the noise can resolve. This is a property of the
*experiment*, not of the optimizer, and is therefore assessable before fitting.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Study A — matched forward reconstruction

**Task.** Reconstruct four node temperatures of the lumped thermoelectric
assembly from incomplete, noisy exchanger measurements.

**Models.** Two networks, **1,116 trainable parameters each**, identical
architecture and identical initial weights. Both receive exact initial
temperatures and known current-switch times. Only one receives the energy
balances.

**Physics residual.** Per node, automatic differentiation supplies `dT/dt` from
the network; the thermoelectric heat terms (Peltier, Joule, conduction leak)
and the contact and reservoir flows supply the rate the balances require; the
loss penalizes the mean squared difference of the four residual columns, in
K/s. No RK4 temperatures are used as labels.

**Data.** 56 retained noisy readings (σ = 0.02 K), with **6 readings removed** —
both exchanger channels between 17 and 23 s, spanning current turn-off.

**Metrics.** RMSE on the retained rows (against both the noisy values and the
underlying truth); RMSE in the missing interval; RMSE on the two module faces,
which supply no temperature labels after the initial state; and an independent
whole-system energy-rate closure in watts, which evaluates the first-law
consequence of the four balances after training rather than adding a fifth loss
term. Five paired trials.

### 3.2 Study B — distributed inverse property inference

**Physical model.** One homogeneous, oriented thermoelectric leg with
temperature resolved along its length, `T(x, t)`, from the cold face (x = 0) to
the hot face (x = L):

```
E = ρ_e(T)·J + α(T)·dT/dx
q = α(T)·T·J − κ(T)·dT/dx
ρ_m·c_p·dT/dt = −dq/dx + J·E
```

which expands to conduction, distributed Joule heating `ρ_e(T)·J²`, and a
Thomson term `−τ(T)·J·dT/dx` with `τ(T) = T·dα/dT` by the Kelvin relation, so
the learned Seebeck curve and Thomson coefficient remain thermodynamically
consistent. Interior cells store energy, so `Q_h − Q_c = VI` holds only at
steady state. Face temperatures are dynamic states, not prescribed labels,
which is what allows terminal measurements to inform hidden material functions.

**Unknown.** The electrical resistivity curve `ρ_e(T)` is represented by three
knots and evaluated over 285–315 K. The fitted trajectories span approximately
294.83–305.21 K, leaving about 19.62 K of the 30 K evaluation interval, roughly
two-thirds, outside the training-temperature range. Reported aggregate curve
error therefore mixes interpolation with extrapolation and does not show that
the detailed curve is uniquely identified throughout 285–315 K. All other
properties and boundary conditions are known.

**Truth.** A separate 25-node spatial discretization, SSPRK3 time integration,
and a smooth cubic resistivity law — a different grid, integrator, and basis
from the inference model.

**Observations.** Noisy face temperatures and terminal voltage under zero,
positive, and negative current (σ = 0.01 K, 10 µV).

**Estimators.** (i) an inverse PINN, and (ii) a strengthened conventional fit
with multiple initial guesses and shrinkage-plus-curvature priors. Both
regularized methods receive the same explicit prior weights, so the comparison
does not reduce to "one method has a smoothness penalty."

**Design.** Ten paired trials sharing each noisy dataset across methods. After
fitting, each inferred curve is frozen and inserted into the *same* conventional
forward solver to predict a withheld +0.4 A, 20 K-lift condition, scoring
terminal-voltage RMSE.

### 3.3 Reliability audits

**Training and curve-shape audit.** Three neural seeds at epoch budgets of 600,
1,200 and 2,400 use physics-loss weight 10. That weight was selected using trial
0 and then frozen before trials 1 and 2, so every three-trial adequacy summary
below includes one development trial and two post-freeze trials. This audit is
separate from the ten-paired-trial inverse-PINN comparison, which used physics
weight 1. The audit tracks recovered
curve amplitude relative to truth, the physical residual RMS as a fraction of
the nominal temperature-rate RMS, and the normalized observation loss. Two gates
are scored: a *truth-blind operational* gate (residual ratio ≤ 0.25, observation
loss ≤ 2.0) that a practitioner could evaluate without knowing the answer, and a
*curve-shape* gate (amplitude ratio within 0.75–1.5) that requires the truth.

**Observation-sufficiency gate.** Before fitting, the noise-normalized,
nuisance-profiled sensitivity matrix for the three resistivity coefficients is
decomposed and each direction is tested against the declared sensor precision.
Four observation sets are compared, and each is fit anyway so that post-fit
quality can be compared against the pre-fit verdict.

**Independent-truth benchmark.** A three-trial comparison of unregularized and
matched-penalty versions of both estimators against the same mismatched truth,
scoring an in-support property-and-transfer gate.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Study A — physics wins where it matters, and loses where it doesn't

Ratios of data-only error to physics-informed error, five paired trials:

| Quantity | Physics-informed | Data-only | Ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Retained noisy rows (RMSE) | 0.01943 K | **0.01747 K** | 0.90x |
| Training time | 15.06 s | **3.95 s** | 0.26x |
| Truth under the retained rows | **0.00679 K** | 0.01329 K | 1.96x |
| Missing interval, both sensors | **0.00970 K** | 0.07988 K | 8.24x |
| Whole-system energy-rate closure | **0.1328 W** | 16.4936 W | 124x |
| Never-instrumented module faces | **0.00710 K** | 2.19372 K | 309x |

Read the first two rows first. **By the only metric available without ground
truth — agreement with the data it was given — the data-only network is the
better model**, and it trains four times faster. It is also wrong by 2.19 K on
the module faces and out by 16.5 W on the energy books. It fit the noise and
learned nothing about the system underneath.

The physics-informed network wins every physics metric in **5 of 5** trials.
Reductions are 87.86% (missing interval), 99.68% (hidden faces), and 99.19%
(energy-rate imbalance).

For reference, the forward distributed PINN on the 1-D leg reaches 0.006420 K
internal-field RMSE and 0.015116 K maximum error without finite-volume
temperature labels.

### 4.2 Study B — lower average property error, inconsistent per-trial transfer

| Metric | Inverse PINN | Conventional fit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean resistivity-curve relative RMSE, same ten paired trials | **1.7537%** | 4.6639% |
| Trials with lower curve error | **9 / 10** | 1 / 10 |
| Mean withheld-condition voltage RMSE | **8.6405 µV** | 11.0188 µV |
| Trials with lower voltage error | 5 / 10 | 5 / 10 |

The corresponding conventional mean over all 20 available conventional trials
is 5.0747%; it is a separate all-20 summary and is not substituted into the
paired comparison. The property-recovery advantage within the paired ten is
consistent. The predictive advantage is not:
the PINN's lower *average* voltage error comes with only a 5-of-10 per-trial
record, so it should be reported as a mean improvement rather than a reliable
per-trial win.

### 4.3 The advantage survives a mismatched truth, and is not the explicit prior

Three paired trials against the 25-node / SSPRK3 / cubic truth, scoring the
in-support property-and-transfer gate:

| Estimator | Passes | Mean property RMSE |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inverse PINN | **3 / 3** | 1.68% |
| Inverse PINN + matched prior | **3 / 3** | 1.69% |
| Conventional fit | 1 / 3 | 7.11% |
| Conventional fit + matched prior | 1 / 3 | 8.20% |

Giving the conventional estimator the identical curvature penalty at the
identical weight does not close the gap (7.11% → 8.20%, still 1 of 3). Whatever
stabilizes the PINN here is implicit neural and field regularization, not a
penalty term anyone could add to the comparator. Three trials is a small sample.

### 4.4 A falling loss does not mean the physics converged

Three seeds per budget, physics weight 10; the summary includes development
trial 0, which selected that weight, plus post-freeze trials 1 and 2:

| Epochs | Curve amplitude vs truth | Physical residual ratio | Observation loss | Operational gate | Curve shape |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 600 | 37.9% | 50.7% | 1.049 | 0 / 3 | 0 / 3 |
| 1,200 | 74.2% | 33.2% | **1.323** | 0 / 3 | 2 / 3 |
| 2,400 | 96.5% | 21.2% | 0.820 | 3 / 3 | 2 / 3 |

Two findings. First, **the observation loss is not monotone**: it is *lower* at
600 epochs (1.049) than at 1,200 (1.323). A practitioner watching the data-fit
metric — the one always available — would rank 600 epochs at least as good as
1,200, when in fact the recovered curve has 38% of its true amplitude and the
PDE residual is still half the nominal temperature-rate scale. Only the physical
residual, falling monotonically 50.7% → 33.2% → 21.2%, tracks real progress.

Second, at 2,400 epochs **3 of 3 seeds pass the truth-blind operational gate but
only 2 of 3 recover the curve shape**. Satisfying every check available without
the answer does not imply the material law was recovered.

### 4.5 A good fit cannot certify that the data were sufficient

Four observation sets, ranked before fitting and then fit anyway:

| Observation set | Pre-fit rank | Verdict | PINN post-fit curve error |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Zero, positive and negative current + voltage | 3 / 3 | permitted | 1.80% |
| Positive current, temperatures + voltage | **2 / 3** | **rejected** | **1.73%** |
| Positive current, face temperatures only | 0 / 3 | rejected | 47.23% |
| Passive relaxation only | 0 / 3 | rejected (structural) | no fit attempted |

Row two is the central result. **The rejected observation set produced a lower
post-fit curve error than the accepted one** (1.73% vs 1.80%), with a better
observation loss (0.698 vs 0.783) and comparable held-out voltage error
(1.0e-5 vs 9e-6). Every diagnostic computable after fitting says it is the
better run; the pre-fit rank gate says one of three coefficient directions is
unresolvable at the declared precision, and the result is discarded.

Row three shows the contrast: at rank 0/3 the PINN visibly fails at 47% error,
so insufficiency sometimes *does* announce itself in the fit. Since it sometimes
does not, fit quality cannot be used to decide which situation you are in. Row
four is the degenerate limit: passive relaxation has zero sensitivity to
resistivity (infinite condition number), so no estimate is attempted.

## 5. Discussion

**What the physics buys.** When the quantity of interest is not measured, the
governing equations are the only thing connecting it to the data. Study A
quantifies that: two orders of magnitude on unmeasured face temperatures and on
energy closure, repeatably. The cost is transparent — 3.8x training time, and
slightly worse agreement with the noisy rows, which is the correct behavior for
a model that declines to fit noise.

**Where the inverse PINN beats a conventional estimator.** On a function-valued
unknown under model mismatch, with matched explicit priors, the PINN was more
accurate and more stable. This is the regime the repository argues PINNs are
for: the lumped ODE problems already have fast, accurate conventional solvers,
and a PINN becomes meaningful when the unknown is a field or a function. The
honest qualifier is that predictive transfer was a coin flip per trial even
where average accuracy improved.

**Two failure modes that require separate gates.** Sections 4.4 and 4.5 identify
failures that no loss curve can reveal: under-converged physics behind a
non-monotone observation loss, and a stable, accurate-looking fit from an
observation set that cannot identify the target. The mitigations are cheap and
independent of the optimizer — monitor the physical residual against a
declared threshold, and run a pre-fit sensitivity-rank gate against the declared
sensor precision before committing to an experiment. That gate is also a design
tool: it says which measurements to add.

**Limitations.**

- Everything is synthetic; no hardware calibration, sensor drift model, or
  measured noise covariance is used.
- Truth and inference share the continuum equations even where the
  discretization and basis differ, so this is model-mismatch robustness rather
  than physics discovery.
- Trial counts are small (5, 10, and 3), with correspondingly wide uncertainty.
- Study A compares one physics-informed network against one data-only network of
  matched size. It is not a general claim about architectures, and the data-only
  baseline has no hidden-state prior or regularizer.
- No forward-solver speedup is claimed; the conventional solvers are faster.
- One leg geometry, one property family released at a time.
- The 1.7537% property error covers 285–315 K even though training temperatures
  span only about 294.83–305.21 K. Roughly two-thirds of that evaluation interval
  is extrapolative; this is not a claim that two-thirds of the error magnitude
  is extrapolation or that the full curve is uniquely identified.
- Conductivity `κ(T)` is the cautionary case: from face temperatures and voltage
  alone the frozen inverse PINN does not recover it reliably, and adding
  idealized heat-rate observations reduces its maximum knot error to about
  0.0515.

## 6. Conclusion

In matched comparisons, embedding thermoelectric energy balances in a neural
network reduced error on never-instrumented temperatures by 99.68% and
whole-system energy imbalance by 99.19%, in every trial, while a data-only
network of identical size fit the supplied noisy data slightly better. On a
temperature-dependent resistivity curve inferred from terminal measurements
under a deliberately mismatched truth, an inverse PINN achieved 1.75% mean curve
error against 4.66% for a strengthened conventional estimator, winning 9 of 10
paired trials, though withheld-condition prediction improved only on average.
Two audits then bound those claims: a non-monotone observation loss concealed
badly under-converged physics, and a pre-fit-rejected observation set yielded
the best-looking fit in the study. The practical conclusion is that
physics-informed inference is worth its cost precisely where data are absent,
and that it is only deployable alongside pre-fit identifiability and physical-
residual gates, because the loss it minimizes cannot certify either.

```bash
python3 -m thermotwin.forward_reconstruction_comparison        # Study A
python3 -m thermotwin.distributed_profile_coverage             # Study B
python3 -m thermotwin.distributed_independent_validation       # mismatched truth
python3 -m thermotwin.distributed_pinn_training_audit          # training audit
python3 -m thermotwin.distributed_observation_identifiability  # sufficiency gate
python3 -m thermotwin.reports.pinn_trust_and_limits            # summary figure
```

`requirements-pinn.txt`. Every study is seeded and writes a PNG with same-stem
JSON and plain-text sidecars. Detailed walkthroughs are
`FORWARD_RECONSTRUCTION_COMPARISON.md`, `DISTRIBUTED_CONSTITUTIVE_INFERENCE.md`,
`DISTRIBUTED_PROFILE_COVERAGE.md`, `DISTRIBUTED_INDEPENDENT_VALIDATION.md`,

with AI coding assistance.
